Battle of the Bands Mechanics

After the Hush, Belenon heals the only way it knows how: with music. Ten guilds are competing in the Gale for the Seat of Breath. Every band writes songs, picks up instruments, catches its breath on the Sacred Winds, and takes the stage. The guilds pair up around four shared resources, so each one plays its own way without walling itself off.

Working reference for the set's design. Synced with mechanics.md. Last updated 15 October 2026.

At a glance

The set has 290 cards (100 commons, 105 uncommons, 65 rares, 20 mythics, Etudes included) in a 15-card booster: 8 commons, an Etude slot, 3 uncommons, a rare or mythic, and two double-faced card slots. It has two layers. Set-wide mechanics appear in every color. Guild themes sit in four groups, each built on one resource several guilds value: Sagas, Join the band, breath, and battles. Guild themes are printed in plain wording with no ability words; their names live only in guides like this one.

Reminder text
  • Only the set's new mechanics print reminder text: breathe, compose, Join the band, and Contests.
  • Returning mechanics never do (scry, surveil, prowess, mentor, amass, crew, basic landcycling, flashback, convoke, Treasure), even at common.
  • Mythics print no reminder text at all, even for the new mechanics.
  • No Saga prints reminder text. The Saga rules are taught once, below.
How Sagas work

Sagas are everywhere in this set (every Etude, and the back face of every Saga creature), so their rules are taught here once and no Saga prints reminder text.

  • As a Saga enters, put a lore counter on it. Then the chapter with that number triggers (I, then II, then III).
  • After your draw step each turn, add another lore counter, which triggers the next chapter.
  • Once the last chapter has triggered and resolved, sacrifice the Saga.
  • Adding a lore counter some other way triggers the next chapter too; removing one triggers nothing.

Set-wide mechanics

These carry the set's core loop. Musicians in your deck compose, pulling Etudes out of your sideboard, and every Etude breathes. Real artists perform until they transform into their songs. Instruments join the band with a Musician of their own. And the bands meet head to head in Contests.

Compose

Keyword actionAll colorsDecided

On Belenon, inspiration arrives on the Sacred Winds. Compose works exactly like Strixhaven's learn, except it finds an Etude: you reach outside the game, which in Limited means your sideboard, and bring one into your hand.

Compose. (You may reveal an Etude card you own from outside the game and put it into your hand, or discard a card to draw a card.)

How it plays
  • It's never dead. With no Etude to fetch, you rummage instead.
  • Every Etude breathes, so compose is also the most dependable way to gain breaths. It limits itself: you can only cast Etudes that share a color with your deck.
  • It's in every color, because every genre writes songs. Classical leans on it hardest, and the Breath guilds' gold enablers compose too.
  • Never on a common that also breathes: a common carries at most one new mechanic.

Etudes

Enchantment — Saga EtudeAll ten guildsDecided

An Etude is a plain, single-faced, hybrid Saga, usually of three chapters, named for a practice piece in its guild's genre: a scale exercise, a rhyme drill, a vocal warm-up. It plays out over a few turns, then it's sacrificed. An étude is a piece written for practice, which makes Etudes Belenon's answer to Strixhaven's Lessons.

[A practice piece] — Enchantment — Saga Etude

I — Breathe, then scry 2. (Gain the breath of each of this card's colors. Breaths are never lost.)
II — …   III — …

How it plays
  • Chapter I always breathes. On common Etudes it's exactly "Breathe, then scry 2," and common Etudes never count breaths. Uncommon and rare Etudes breathe on chapter I and may count breaths later.
  • There are 20 Etudes, all in the main set: 10 commons (one per guild) fill each booster's Etude slot, 5 uncommons (the enemy pairs) and 5 rares (the allied pairs) sit on the regular sheets. No mythic Etude.
  • Etudes should be good enough to main-deck, but not so good that nobody leaves them in the sideboard for compose.
  • "Etude" appears in rules text only in compose and the subtype. Anything that keys off Etudes says Saga instead (Whenever you cast a Saga spell), so the cards work outside this format.
  • Every Etude carries a metronome icon at the left of its name bar, the way every Lesson carries a scroll and quill, and uses the split two-color Saga frame.
  • No Saga prints reminder text; the Saga rules are taught once, up front (see How Sagas work).

Breath

Keyword action + player resourceHybrid cards breatheDecided

Music on Belenon is breath carried on the Sacred Winds, tinted by mana color. Each player has five breaths, one per color. A hybrid card that breathes gives you the breath of each of its colors, and payoffs across the set count how many you have.

Breathe. (Gain the breath of each of this card's colors. Breaths are never lost.)

… for each breath you have  ·  If you have three or more breaths, …  ·  Whenever you gain a breath, …

How it plays
  • Only hybrid cards breathe. A card gives white and black breath; the card's colors are its breaths. That's the whole rule. Hybrid gives, gold pays off.
  • Never lost; repeats do nothing. Like ascend or the Ring tempting you, a breath stays for the rest of the game, and breathing a color you already have gains nothing. Track them on a helper card with five boxes.
  • Counting: the standard threshold at common and uncommon is three, a two-color deck plus one neighbor's hybrid card. Payoffs are fine at 2, great at 4; 5 is rare.
  • "Whenever you gain a breath" triggers once for each breath gained, and only while the card is on the battlefield, so at most five times a game.
  • Access: a guild can cast hybrid cards from the seven pairs that share a color with it. Every Etude's chapter I, the 10 common and 10 uncommon hybrid cards, and the rare Etudes breathe: about 2.4 breath cards per pack.
  • No card both breathes and counts breaths, unless the count comes later through a separate trigger. Uncommon hybrids do exactly that; common hybrids are pure enablers.

Precedent: domain (counting distinct things from 1 to 5), ascend and the Ring (a permanent player-level status), party (distinct categories up to a cap), and energy (a player resource tracked on a helper card).

Watch in playtesting
  • How fast Breath decks reach three breaths. If most Soul, Jazz and Blues decks get there by turn 4, raise a few thresholds to four.

Saga creatures

Double-faced cardsAll colorsDecided

The real-world legends who play the Gale take the stage as themselves. Each one is a legendary creature that can transform into one of their real songs, a terminal Saga. When the song ends, the artist is gone, so when to transform is the real decision.

[Artist, Title] — Legendary Creature — Human Musician
[abilities]
{cost}: Exile [this], then return [it] to the battlefield transformed under [its] owner's control. Activate only as a sorcery.

// [Their real song] — Enchantment — Saga

How it plays
  • Transforming works like a sacrifice: you lose the body, counters, and Instruments, so the song has to be worth more than the creature.
  • The Saga enters (exile and return), so chapter I fires immediately and Country's enchantment payoffs trigger.
  • They're main-deck cards, not Etudes, so compose can't find them.
  • There are 31: 10 commons (one per guild) in the first double-faced slot, and 10 gold uncommon legends, 10 gold rare headliners, and the mythic Amadeus in the second. Every real-world legend is one, except Salieri.
  • Chapters follow real Sagas: tokens on about a quarter of them, more removal and some ramp, and team pumps only as a guild's finale.

Join the band

KeywordAll colorsDecided

Instruments are Equipment, and the best of them bring a player. Join the band works exactly like Final Fantasy's job select: the Equipment arrives with a 1/1 Musician already holding it, a wind spirit that takes shape to play.

Join the band (When this Equipment enters, create a 1/1 colorless Musician creature token, then attach this to it.)
Equipped creature gets +N/+N [and has …].
Equip {N}

How it plays
  • It counts as a creature slot. Count the token plus the bonus as its effective P/T.
  • There are 15 Join the band cards (8 C, 3 U, 4 R). Instruments are plain Artifact — Equipment, and payoffs say "Equipment."
  • When the Equipment is gone, the Musician stays. The 1/1 colorless Musician is also the set's universal token.
  • The Join the band group's three guilds each use it their own way: Punk attacks with equipped creatures, Rock uses Instruments to reach power 4, and Metal feeds the Musician to its sacrifice outlets.

Contests

Battle — ContestUncommon+Decided

The set's Battle of the Bands. A Contest is a new battle subtype: an opponent protects it and you attack it, but the crowd doesn't care how hard you hit. Each time your creatures connect, you reveal cards from your library and remove defense counters equal to their total mana value. Win, and you cast the back face: the prize.

[Name of the contest] — Battle — Contest · defense N
(An opponent protects this Contest. Combat damage doesn't remove its defense counters. Instead, whenever creatures deal combat damage to it, reveal that many cards from the top of your library, remove defense counters equal to their total mana value, then surveil that many. When it's defeated, exile it, then cast it transformed.)
[front effect]

// [The prize] — [back face]

How it plays
  • Revealing doesn't move the cards, so the surveil looks at exactly the cards you revealed. Stacking a big spell on top before combat (scry, surveil) is the cypher prep, and that's Rap's edge.
  • Noncombat damage works the normal battle way: each point removes a defense counter, so burn hits Contests. Life loss doesn't. Damage that can hit a Contest reads as a performance (a solo, a riff, pyrotechnics), never as destruction.
  • Defense 3 on every uncommon Contest plays like a Siege at defense 4–5. The mythic Final Cypher is 4, and The Gale is 5.
  • Ten gold uncommon Contests, one per guild against a rival: the front answers the rival's strategy, and the back pays off your own guild. Matchups: Classical vs. Punk, Rap vs. Blues, Metal vs. Soul, Rock vs. Jazz, Country vs. Electronic, Soul vs. Rap, Jazz vs. Metal, Electronic vs. Rock, Punk vs. Country, Blues vs. Classical.
  • Rap also gets two mono-colored battles (Open-Mic Night and Grudge Match) and the mythic The Final Cypher. The Gale, whose prize is the Seat of Breath, is the final.

Precedent: the Siege battles of March of the Machine. Contests restate the protector and transform rules, and add rule 310.13 for their own combat-damage rule.

Saxophone vertical cycle

Vertical cycleRed EquipmentDecided

One saxophone at each rarity, all red Equipment. The bigger the horn, the bigger the numbers, with one exception: the mythic Soprano, the War Anthem horn and the Gale's prize, is the smallest and cheapest, with all its power in its ability.

  • Common · Alto Saxophone (CR04): the only one with Join the band; +1/+1 and prowess.
  • Uncommon · Tenor Saxophone (UR09): attaches as it enters; +2/+1, and trample while the creature's power is 4 or greater.
  • Rare · Baritone Saxophone (RR01): +2/+2, and on attack it deals 1 damage to each creature the defending player controls.
  • Mythic · Soprano Saxophone (MR01, legendary): , +1/+0 and haste, and on attack your creatures get +1/+1.

Returning mechanics

Existing keywordsNo reminder textDecided

Real Play Booster sets carry a tail of small returning mechanics, so Belenon has one too. Each sits on a card with no other set mechanic, mostly in place of plain text, and none prints reminder text. Scry and surveil handle the top of the library throughout.

  • Basic landcycling , one common per color. It smooths draws in a slow, Saga-heavy format and finds a basic for a Breath splash.
  • Flashback for Jazz's replays and Electronic's loops: the encore.
  • Convoke: the crowd joins in, and Join the band and amass make plenty of small bodies.
  • Treasure, for Breath decks' fixing, Rap's royalties, and Punk's smash-and-grab.

Guild groups

Each guild should play like its genre, but no guild is walled off. The ten guilds sit in four groups, and each group shares one resource that its guilds use in different ways, as in Innistrad and Modern Horizons 2. Every theme is built on something its colors have done before, printed in plain wording with no ability word. Hybrid cards give (they breathe), and gold cards pay off; every payoff is still playable without its theme.

The Sagas group · Classical, Country, Electronic

Etudes and Saga creatures' back faces put Sagas everywhere. Classical casts them, Country cares about enchantments entering, and Electronic about their final chapters and lore counters.

Cadenza Conservatory · Classical ·

Etudes and compose

Sagas groupCommon+Decided

Classical practices. It leans on compose harder than anyone, and its payoffs trigger whenever you cast a Saga. In this set the only castable Sagas are the Etudes, so every practice piece you fetch feeds the conservatory.

Whenever you cast a Saga spell, …

Saga spells you cast cost less to cast.

How it plays
  • Compose is the engine; Saga cost reduction (Cadenza Accompanist, and a rare headliner) speeds it up.
  • The rules text says Saga, never Etude, so the cards work outside this format.
  • Classical is a slow guild with a common value wall, and taps blockers down rather than racing.

Precedent: Strixhaven's Lessons and learn, and "whenever you cast" spell payoffs.

Twangwood Jamboree · Country ·

Storytelling

Sagas groupCommon+Decided

Country tells a story one verse at a time. Every enchantment that enters under your control is a new verse, and Country's cards pay off each one, mostly with +1/+1 counters.

Whenever an enchantment you control enters, …

How it plays
  • Etudes count, and so does every Saga creature's back face, because transforming makes the Saga enter.
  • +1/+1 counters are Country's second theme, so many payoffs grow the band.
  • "Storytelling" is just what we call the group; the cards print the trigger plainly.

Precedent: constellation in Theros, and Selesnya's enchantment decks.

Loopwerk Collective · Electronic ·

Build-up and drop

Sagas groupCommon+ (drops at uncommon+)Decided

A track climbs for minutes while everyone waits, then the bass drops and the floor explodes. In Electronic, every Saga is the build-up and its final chapter is the drop. When a Saga you control gets there, your drop cards fire, and the floor lights up for a turn.

Whenever the final chapter ability of a Saga you control triggers, [temporary effect].

If you control a Saga, …

How it plays
  • Drop payoffs are temporary: a pump, evasion, a burst of mana, or a one-shot like recursion. Time your big attack for the turn a Saga finishes. They start at uncommon.
  • It says "triggers," not "resolves," so it fires as the final chapter goes on the stack, before the Saga is sacrificed.
  • Lore counters speed things up: Loopwerk Producer and Warehouse Sound Tech add them, and Rewind Selecta loops finished Sagas back to hand.
  • At common it's simple "if you control a Saga" checks.

Precedent: Historian's Boon, Narci, Fable Singer, and Tom Bombadil, WotC's final-chapter payoffs; Keldon Warcaller for adding lore counters at common.

The Join the band group · Punk, Rock 'n' Roll, Metal

Instruments and their Musician tokens are the shared resource. Punk attacks with equipped creatures, Rock uses gear to reach power 4, and Metal sacrifices the tokens.

Anarkos Riot · Punk ·

Attacking with equipped creatures

Join the band groupCommon+Decided

Punk is social change and counterculture: everybody grabs an instrument and storms the stage. Its cards reward equipped creatures attacking, hand out mentor-style counters, and pass the Instrument to a new member.

Whenever this creature attacks, if it's equipped, …

Whenever this creature attacks, put a +1/+1 counter on each of up to X other target attacking creatures, where X is the number of breaths you have.

How it plays
  • Mentor, the returning keyword, appears on two cards, including the Tambourine.
  • Passing Instruments (Picket-Line Captain, Anarkos Busker) keeps the gear moving to fresh attackers.
  • Punk doesn't sacrifice: white never sacrifices artifacts. The two red smash cards (Break a Leg, Rioter's Manifesto) take "an artifact or creature," so Metal uses them too.
  • Punk's agitators, rioters and dropouts have the creature type Rebel.
Stadiax Tour · Rock 'n' Roll ·

Ferocious

Join the band groupCommon+Decided

Turn it up to eleven. A Rock 'n' Roll card gets better when the headliner is out front: a creature with power 4 or greater.

If you control a creature with power 4 or greater, …

How it plays
  • Equipment is just one way to get there. A 1/1 Musician holding a Six-String Axe is a 4/2 out of the box.
  • On a spell, the condition is checked as it resolves.
  • "Ferocious" is just what we call the group; the cards print the condition plainly.

Precedent: Temur's ferocious in Khans of Tarkir, such as Feed the Clan and Crater's Claws.

Headbanga Mob · Metal ·

Amass Fanatics

Join the band groupExisting keywordAll raritiesDecided

The Headbanga Mob is a front for Phyrexians, and their fanbase is a literal cult. Metal amasses Fanatics, then sacrifices creature tokens, both Fanatic Armies and Musician tokens, for value.

Amass Fanatics N.

Sacrifice a creature token: …

How it plays
  • It's the existing amass keyword with Fanatic as the creature type, so it prints no reminder text in this set.
  • Sacrifice outlets and token payoffs carry the theme, along with the red smash cards, which take "an artifact or creature."
  • The Mob is hidden, then revealed: commons look like ordinary Mob members, and the mythics reveal Phyrexians (Vorhaxa, and The Still Engine, a Vehicle the cult drives).

Precedent: amass from War of the Spark, and amass Orcs from The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth.

The Breath group · Soul, Jazz, Blues

Every guild's hybrid cards breathe, but these three pay it off. Soul is the voice, Jazz the horns, and Blues the harmonica.

Gospelle Choir · Soul ·

The voice

Breath groupCommon+Decided

Soul sings from the chest. Breath sets the size of its life gain and drain, and its best cards trigger whenever you gain a breath.

… you gain 1 life for each breath you have.

Whenever you gain a breath, each opponent loses N life and you gain N life.

How it plays
  • White gains life and black drains it; three breaths turn a small drain into a big one.
  • Gospelle Deacon finds a hybrid card in your library, so Soul can line up its breath triggers.
  • Soul is one of the slow guilds, with a common value wall.
The Improvisari · Jazz ·

The horns: extra modes

Breath groupCommon+Decided

Jazz improvises over the changes. Its modal spells and creatures let you pick one option, and with enough breath you play both.

Choose one. If you have three or more breaths, choose both instead.
• …   • …

How it plays
  • Modal cards are never dead: early you pick the mode you need, later you get both.
  • Improvisari Sideman makes modal spells cheaper; breath-gated copies and impulse draw live at rare.
  • Flashback is Jazz's replay, the encore.

Precedent: entwine and escalate, which let you pay to choose every mode; here breath unlocks it instead of mana.

Deltari Wanderers · Blues ·

The harmonica: growth and thresholds

Breath groupCommon+Decided

Hard times, sung through. Blues creatures grow as you gain breaths, and its spells unlock something extra at three. It plays at instant speed, with plenty of flash.

If you have three or more breaths, …

Whenever you gain a breath, put a +1/+1 counter on this creature.

How it plays
  • Flash creatures that tap or bounce let Blues answer on the opponent's turn.
  • Deltari Rambler, the gold enabler, composes, so Blues reaches its Etudes and their breaths.
  • Blues is one of the slow guilds, with a common value wall.
The Battles group · Rap

Every guild has a Contest, but Rap lives for them: it hits players and battles, stacks its library for the reveal, and cashes in when a battle falls.

Lyriq Crew · Rap ·

Cyphers

Battles groupCommon+Decided

Rap out-talks the opponent. Its creatures reward combat damage to a player or battle, its blue and black library stacking (scry, surveil) sets up big Contest reveals, and from uncommon up it pays off whenever a battle you control is defeated.

Whenever this creature deals combat damage to a player or battle, …

Whenever a battle you control is defeated, …

How it plays
  • Putting a high-mana-value card on top before combat is the cypher prep: the reveal takes off more defense counters.
  • Rap gets two mono-colored battles (Open-Mic Night in blue, Grudge Match in black) and the mythic Contest The Final Cypher.
  • A battle is defeated about once a game, so defeat triggers stay at uncommon and up.
  • Sampling survives on a few Rap cards: look at the top three of an opponent's library, exile one face down, and cast it as your own.

Precedent: Gonti, Lord of Luxury and Thief of Sanity, for Sampling.

Also in the set

  • A slow-enough format. Common creatures average about 2.5 power and 3.0 toughness, 2-drops are defensive, every color has a 6+ mana common finisher, and haste is rare, so Sagas and Etudes have time to play out.
  • High card power. Commons are Pauper Cube–worthy and rares are power-cube candidates, with the power going into value and flexibility rather than speed.
  • Creature types. People plus class (Creature — Rat Rogue). Performers are Musicians, Punk's are Rebels, and the universal token is a 1/1 colorless Musician.
  • Rivalry. Story only. Amadeus and Salieri feud through names, art, and flavor text, with no mechanic.
  • Retired in the 2026-10-15 rework. Addendum (Classical), Testify (Soul), Jam (Jazz) and Lowdown (Blues) as guild mechanics, and every ability word.
